# P2-14 — `no_std` boundary audit + wasm32-wasip2 lanes

**The audit first, then the lanes it licenses. Landed 2026-08-20; every
acceptance clause met, with one structural deviation (the lanes are steps of
`clippy-and-test`, not new jobs — the check.yml length ratchet is the reason)
and one expectation the audit corrects rather than confirms (the oxc crates
do not declare `no_std`). The boundary itself is
[`no-std-boundary.md`](../no-std-boundary.md); this record is what the task
measured and why the lane looks the way it does.**

## Where the boundary doc lives

The contract says "a committed plan doc" without naming it. It landed as
[`davinci-road/plan/no-std-boundary.md`](../no-std-boundary.md), beside
`folio-format.md` and `walk-baseline.md` — the other documents that CI
consumers and review points cite by path.

## The lane is a step extension, and the ratchet is why

The contract reads "add the CI jobs to `.github/workflows/check.yml`". A new
job costs ~18 lines (checkout, toolchain, sticky cache, two builds, plus the
`test-report` `needs:` entry), and check.yml is a 699-line file already over
the 350-line ratchet, whose `OverLimitFileGrew` rule fails **any** net growth
(`tools/moon/cmd/source_file_lengths`, enforced per PR by `check:ci`). A new
job would also have forced the `NEEDED_JOBS` deep-equal and all six hardcoded
"18 needed jobs" oracles in
`tests/tooling/github-workflows-check-gate.test.ts` to churn.

So TS-24 rides the job that already holds the full-CLI `std` lane:
`clippy-and-test` gained `targets: wasm32-wasip2` on its toolchain step and
one step ("Davinci no_std portability lanes (TS-24)") running both builds.
Cost: 4 lines, funded net-zero by removing the 4 intra-step blank lines the
`coverage` job alone carried (no other job separates setup steps with
blanks). Required-ness is inherited — `clippy-and-test` is unconditional and
in `test-report`'s `needs:` — and the placement cannot silently dissolve:
`tests/tooling/davinci-portability-lane.test.ts` pins the unconditional job,
the `targets:` input, the exact two commands, and the
`#![no_std]`/`extern crate alloc` attributes on both crates. The
core-compile/full-CLI separation the contract asks for is therefore two lanes
in one job: the TS-24 step covers exactly the two claim crates, everything
else in the job stays `std`.

The `--no-default-features` build runs **with** `--target wasm32-wasip2` (the
TS-24 row read literally: the same command plus the flag), keeping both lanes
on the 32-bit target so the size-assert guards are exercised in both feature
configurations.

## What the audit measured

- **Three accepted `std` edges**, not one: the proc-macro
  (`vize_davinci_derive`, recorded at P2-4 and confirmed), the single library
  edge (`vize_carton`, `std` by charter), and the `davinci-opt` **bin
  target** — which the contract never mentions but which
  `cargo build --target wasm32-wasip2` compiles, so leaving it out of the
  boundary would have left the lane proving something the doc did not say.
- **`std` types cross the boundary by re-export**: `vize_carton::FxHashMap`
  is `std::collections::HashMap` under the Fx hasher; the folio map sections
  hold it without naming `std`. The audit states this so a `std::`-grep
  auditor is not misled.
- **The oxc crates are not `no_std`**: none of the six in the closure carry a
  marker at rev `fc702c1f`. The plan's step phrased them as expected
  `no_std + alloc` supporters; the measured answer is no, and the lane only
  needs them to compile for wasip2 (they do).
- **salsa is not in `Cargo.lock` at all** (0 occurrences). The plan lists it
  as a `std`-bound resident-tier dependency; there is no edge to accept yet.
- **The feature seam decision**: neither crate grows one. A `std` feature
  would invert an unconditionally-`no_std` design, so the audit names the
  trigger (the first real consumer-driven seam) instead of planting a
  decorative one. The flag's vacuity was proven, not asserted: the second
  lane build is a 0.13s no-op rebuild of the first.

## What the 32-bit lane proves about the guards

All guarded size asserts in both crates (3 sites in `vize_davinci`, 15 types
across 7 sites in `vize_s2`) compile **out** on wasip2 — the lane is the
standing proof the guards are load-bearing, because at 4-byte pointers the
64-bit figures are wrong and an unguarded pointer-dependent assert breaks the
build. The deliberately unguarded `NodeId == 4` / `Option<NodeId> == 4` pair
(`vize_davinci/src/id.rs:39-40`) is **evaluated** on the lane, checking the
niche at the width where it matters. The audit doc carries the full table.

## Verified locally vs. deferred to CI

Verified locally: both lane commands green for wasm32-wasip2 via the P2-4
sysroot overlay (upstream `rust-std-1.95.0-wasm32-wasip2` for rustc commit
`59807616e` over the nix toolchain — the flake still carries only
`wasm32-unknown-unknown`); check.yml still parses and still counts 699 lines;
all 28 workflow tooling tests green (`github-workflows-check`,
`github-workflows-check-gate`, `github-workflows-vue-parity`, and the new
pin file); markdown through oxfmt. No Rust source changed, so clippy/fmt are
untouched surfaces.

Deferred: the lane itself is first exercised by the first CI run of this PR —
CI installs the target through `dtolnay/rust-toolchain`'s `targets:` input,
which no local toolchain can rehearse. Normal for a CI-lane task, recorded
rather than hidden.
